The mindset reset

Everything starts here. Your pipeline is empty because your mind hasn't accepted what this job actually demands. Until you do, no script, no training, no tool will save you. These are the brutal realities you must internalize before you dial a single number.

"Fanatical prospectors are relentless. They prospect when they don't feel like it, when it's inconvenient, and when someone tells them to stop."

— Jeb Blount
The 3 brutal truths — internalize all 3 or nothing works
Day 1 — non-negotiable foundation
Truth 1: An empty pipeline is the only real failure
  • Write this on an index card and tape it to your monitor right now: "My pipeline is my salary. I fill it every single day."
  • Every morning before you touch your phone, say it out loud. This is not motivational fluff — it's neural reprogramming. Repetition builds automatic behaviour.
  • When you feel like skipping a calling block because you have "enough in the pipeline," that feeling is a lie. The 30-Day Rule means what you don't do today will hollow out your results in 90 days.
  • Track your pipeline visually — a whiteboard with stages, a kanban board, anything physical you see daily. When it's thin, the visual discomfort should feel uncomfortable enough to drive action.
  • Outcome: Prospecting stops being optional. It becomes as non-negotiable as showing up. You wouldn't skip eating — don't skip prospecting.
Truth 2: Prospecting is interrupting — own it
  • You are calling someone who did not ask you to call. This is a fact. Stop apologising for it in your tone, your opener, or your body language. You are not a nuisance — you are a professional with something valuable.
  • Before you dial, ask yourself: "Do I genuinely believe my product/service can help this person?" If you hesitate, you have a product belief problem, not a prospecting problem. Fix the belief first.
  • The word "just" is banned from your vocabulary on calls. "I'm just calling to..." signals low confidence. Replace it: "I'm calling because..." — assertive, clear, confident.
  • Stop saying "sorry to bother you" or "I know you're busy." These phrases validate every reason they have to hang up. You're not bothering them — you're offering them something. Act like it.
  • Outcome: Your voice changes. You dial with confidence instead of dread. Prospects can hear the difference — and they respond to it.
Truth 3: Rejection is the job description
  • Set a daily rejection goal. Literally: aim for 20 "no's" per day. When you hit 20, you worked. Celebrate the rejection because it means you showed up and dialled.
  • Rejection is not personal — they rejected the interruption, not you. They don't know you. They rejected the timing, the moment, the context. Stop making it about your worth.
  • After every rejection, write down the exact words used. "Not interested." "We already have someone." "Send me an email." These become your objection library — the most valuable asset you'll build in your first 90 days.
  • The best salespeople in the world get rejected more than average reps. They just dial more. Volume is the cure for rejection sensitivity.
  • Outcome: Rejection stops stinging because you've reframed it. Each "no" is data and a step toward the next "yes." You can't get to yes without going through no.
Build your "why" anchor — the emotional foundation
Day 1–3 · Do this before your first block
The "why" exercise
  • Get a blank sheet of paper. Write "Why do I need to succeed in sales?" at the top. Set a timer for 10 minutes. Write without filtering. Family, money, freedom, proving people wrong, security — all valid.
  • Then narrow it to one sentence: "I do this because ___." That sentence goes on your wall, next to your monitor, visible while you dial.
  • On your worst days — and they're coming — read it before you pick up the phone. Your "why" must be bigger than your discomfort, or you'll quit when it gets hard.
  • Revisit and update it every 90 days. As your income grows, your "why" evolves. Keep it current or it loses its power.
Build a pre-call state ritual (5 minutes, every block)
  • Minute 1–2: Your hype playlist. Not ambient music. Something that puts you in a peak physical and emotional state. Stand up while you listen.
  • Minute 3: Read your "why" out loud. Then say: "I am the best at this. I help people. Today I will make 50 dials."
  • Minute 4–5: Read your script out loud, twice. Not silently — out loud. Your mouth needs to know the words before your brain is fully engaged.
  • Smile before you dial. This is not optional. Record yourself smiling vs. not smiling. Your voice literally sounds different. Warmer, more confident, more trustworthy. Prospects hear the smile.
  • Stand up during your calling block if possible. Your voice carries more authority. Your energy is higher. You move away from avoidance psychology that sitting enables.
Eliminate "getting ready to get ready" — the silent pipeline killer
Week 1 · Most dangerous habit for new reps
The 5 avoidance behaviours disguised as work
  • Research paralysis: Spending 3+ hours researching one prospect before calling is procrastination dressed up as professionalism. You need 90 seconds of research per prospect — maximum. Name, title, one thing about their company. Done. Set a timer.
  • Script perfectionism: Your script will never be perfect. Write a draft today and make 20 calls tomorrow. You will learn more from 20 real calls than from 20 hours of script editing. The script is built in the field, not at a desk.
  • CRM over-logging: During calling blocks, log only: Attempted / Voicemail / Spoke / Outcome. Details come during Platinum Hours. Every minute spent typing during a Golden Hour is a dial you didn't make.
  • Inbox management: Email is not revenue. Checking email during a calling block is avoidance. Set it to check every 2 hours during Golden Hours, not constantly.
  • Social media "research": Scrolling LinkedIn for intel on prospects feels productive. It is mostly not. Get in, get the one fact you need, get out. 90 seconds. Timer on.
  • Action: Set a 90-second research timer per prospect. When it goes off, you dial — ready or not. The call will teach you more than the research ever could.

Jeb's fundamental laws

These aren't suggestions. They are the governing physics of your pipeline. Violate them and you pay the price in 90 days. Follow them and your results become predictable, scalable, and compounding.

The 30-Day Rule
The prospecting you do today will pay off in the next 90 days. What you don't do today will create a gap in your pipeline 30–90 days from now. This lag is invisible — which makes it dangerous. You feel fine today and wonder why you're broke next quarter.
  • Never skip a prospecting day because you "have enough in the pipeline right now." That's the exact moment most reps stop prospecting — and create the future slump they'll blame on the market.
  • When you land a big deal, go straight back to prospecting the same day. Most reps celebrate and go quiet. That silence creates a drought in 90 days. Discipline beats emotion.
  • Mark a wall calendar every day you hit your dial number. Streaks matter. Never break a 2-day miss into a 3-day miss. A 3-day miss becomes a week. A week becomes a habit of not prospecting.
  • Slow months do not come from slow markets — they come from slow prospecting 2–3 months earlier. Always look backwards to diagnose your present, and forward to protect your future.
The Law of Replacement
Every prospect that advances or exits your pipeline must be immediately replaced. Your pipeline has a leak rate. If you don't understand yours, you're flying blind. Add new prospects at a rate greater than your closing ratio or your pipeline will shrink — even when you're closing deals.
  • After every meeting, proposal, or discovery call that moves forward — go back to your list and add 3 new prospects to replace the forward motion. Every advance creates a gap. Fill it now.
  • Calculate your monthly leak rate: (prospects removed or lost) ÷ (total prospects in pipeline). If 30% drop off monthly, you must add 30%+ new ones just to stay flat. Add 40%+ to grow.
  • When a prospect ghosts you after 5+ unanswered touches across 14 days — remove them and replace immediately. Don't hold them in your pipeline. Ghosts inflate your numbers and distort your ratios.
  • Replacement is not a monthly task. It's a daily reflex. Every deal that moves forward, every deal that dies — immediately replaced. Same day.
The Law of Need
The more desperately you need a sale, the less likely you are to get it. Desperation is detectable. Prospects hear it in your vocal pace, feel it in your follow-up frequency, and sense it in your willingness to cave on price. A full pipeline is the only antidote to desperation.
  • The moment you feel desperate, the answer is more prospecting — not more pressure on your existing pipeline. Go make 20 more dials. Add prospects. Cure desperation with abundance.
  • Signs you're operating from need: dropping your price before they ask, calling a prospect 3 times in one day, over-explaining why they should buy, giving discounts in your opener. All desperation signals.
  • Never call a prospect more than once per day across any channel. One touch per day maximum. Any more and you've crossed from persistent into desperate.
  • Negotiate from strength by having 20 other deals moving. When you genuinely don't need this specific deal, your posture and language change — and prospects feel it. They buy from the confident version of you.
The Law of Familiarity
The more familiar a prospect is with you before you call, the less friction you face. A cold call to a total stranger faces maximum resistance. A call to someone who's seen your name twice, connected with you on LinkedIn, and read your email faces far less. Warm the cold call before you make it.
  • 15 minutes before each calling block (during Platinum Hours), send LinkedIn connection requests to your day's prospect list with a brief personal note. Not a pitch — just a name and context.
  • On the call, reference it: "I sent you a connection on LinkedIn yesterday — you may have seen my name." Familiar beats cold every single time.
  • Send a brief email the morning before you call: one sentence about why you're reaching out, one sentence about what you do, a note that you'll call that afternoon. Don't ask for anything. Just prime their recognition.
  • Follow company news, post relevant content in your space, engage with their company LinkedIn posts — all of this builds familiarity before your call. Strategic visibility reduces resistance.
The Three B's — Be Brief. Be Bright. Be Gone.
Prospects are busy and skeptical. Every extra second you stay on the line after your ask increases the chance they hang up. Your opener must be confident, sharp, and lead directly to your ask — in under 30 seconds. Anything more and you're filling silence with self-defeat.
  • Time your opener with a stopwatch. From "Hi [Name]!" to your ask should be under 30 seconds. Practise until you hit it every single time without rushing.
  • Cut every filler word: "um," "so," "basically," "kind of," "just wanted to check in." These broadcast low confidence. Record yourself and count them. Eliminate them one by one.
  • After your ask — stop talking. Silence is your ally, not your enemy. The first person who speaks after the ask is in the weaker position. Train yourself to wait a full 5 seconds. It will feel like forever. Do it anyway.
  • Efficient calls get more respect. Prospects appreciate that you didn't waste their time. That respect translates into a more receptive audience when you call back.
The Law of Consistency — Show Up Every Day
Inconsistency is the enemy of every salesperson. You cannot compensate for 3 days of not prospecting with one monster calling day. The pipeline doesn't work that way. Consistent daily output compounds. Sporadic bursts create feast-and-famine cycles.
  • Elite reps do not have "good weeks" and "bad weeks" for prospecting effort. They have consistent weeks. Their number of dials barely varies. Their results vary, but their effort doesn't.
  • Build a non-negotiable daily minimum: the number of dials you will hit regardless of what else happens. Not a target — a floor. Your floor might be 30 dials. Never go below it. Some days you'll hit 60. Never go below 30.
  • Track your 30-day rolling average of dials. This smooths out the noise of good and bad days and shows you your true consistent output level. That number is your real productivity baseline.
  • The compounding effect: 40 dials a day × 22 working days = 880 dials per month. At an 8% connect rate, that's 70 conversations. At 25% appointment rate, that's 17–18 appointments. Every day you skip cuts into this compounding math.

Protecting your Golden Hours — the exact tactics
Ongoing discipline · Non-negotiable
How to defend your blocks from everyone — including yourself
  • Block 8–10am and 4–6pm in your shared calendar as "Revenue Block — Unavailable." Label them clearly. Decline every non-emergency meeting request that falls inside these windows.
  • When a colleague interrupts: "I'm in a calling block — I'll catch you at 10." No apology, no guilt. You are doing the job that pays your bills. They will understand.

Step 1 — Get attention in the first 5 seconds
The first five seconds determine everything
What you say
"Hi [Name]!" ← warm, upbeat, slight upward inflection — not a question, not flat
The exact mechanics of the opener
  • First name only. "Mr./Ms. [Surname]" telegraphs salesperson instantly. "[First Name]!" sounds like someone they know. The difference is massive.
  • Inflection is critical. Practice saying "Hi [Name]!" in three ways: flat, questioning, and warm-confident. The warm-confident version is right between a statement and a question. Record all three. Use the third one every time.
  • Pause 1 full second after their name. Let them register who's calling. Fill that second with silence, not more words. New reps barrel through this pause — don't.
  • What NOT to say: "Hi, is this [Name]?" (sounds uncertain), "Hi [Name], how are you today?" (sounds like a telemarketer), "Hi [Name], do you have a minute?" (invites rejection before you've said anything).
  • Physical state check: If you dialled while seated and slumped, they can hear it. Stand up, shoulders back, slight smile. This is not optional — it is acoustic performance.
Step 2 — Identify yourself with clarity and confidence
8 seconds · No apology, no qualifier
What you say
"This is [Your Name] with [Company Name]." ← period. Not a question. Not a pitch. Just facts.
The mechanics of self-identification
  • State your name and company once. Clearly. Without rushing. New reps speed up on their company name because they're nervous — this makes it inaudible. Slow down specifically on the company name.
  • Do not add a tagline here: "...with XYZ, the leading provider of..." You'll lose them before the bridge. Save the value framing for step 3.
  • Do not say "I'm just [Name] from [Company]." The word "just" diminishes you. You're not "just" anyone. You're a professional with a purpose.
  • Practice this sentence specifically until you can deliver it calmly when you're nervous, rushed, or getting rejections back-to-back. This is the most stumbled-over sentence in cold calling.
  • Your name and company must be memorable after hearing them once. Slow, clear, and confident delivery makes this possible. Fast, nervous delivery makes them ask "sorry, who was that?" — which you want to avoid.
Step 3 — The bridge: your reason for calling
Make it about them — not you
Bridge template 1 — Pain-based
"I work with [type of companies] to help them [specific outcome]. We've found that [industry] companies typically face [specific pain] — and we've built something that addresses that directly."
Bridge template 2 — Referral or research
"[Mutual contact / I was looking at your company] — [one specific thing you noticed]. I thought there might be a relevant conversation here."
Bridge template 3 — Social proof
"We've recently worked with [similar company name] to [specific outcome they achieved]. Given what you're doing at [their company], I thought this might be worth a conversation."
The rules of the bridge
  • Write 3 bridges for your product today. One pain-based, one outcome-based, one specific-to-them. Rotate them across your calling days to find what resonates best with your ICP.
  • Never say "I wanted to introduce myself." That's about you. The bridge must be entirely about them — their pain, their outcome, their industry, their company.
  • Spend 90 seconds researching each prospect before the call. One LinkedIn glance: What's their title? What does their company do? Any recent news? Use one of these in the bridge to show you're not dialling randomly.
  • The bridge is NOT a pitch. It's a reason why this call makes sense for them. 1–2 sentences maximum. If you're still bridging at 30 seconds, you've turned it into a monologue and lost them.
  • Specificity is everything. "I work with technology companies" is weak. "I work with Series B SaaS companies with 50–200 employees to reduce churn in their first 90 days" is specific. Specificity builds instant credibility.
Step 4 — The ask: the most important sentence on the call
Two choices · Then silence · Non-negotiable
The ask — always two specific options
"I'd love to set up a 20-minute call to [specific purpose]. Are you free Tuesday at 10am or would Wednesday at 2pm work better for you?"
The science of the ask
  • Always offer two specific times. Not "whenever works for you" — this puts all the cognitive burden on them and produces "I'll check and get back to you" (which means never). Two options force a simple binary choice.
  • Keep the commitment small: "20 minutes." Easy to say yes to. You can always extend to 45 if the conversation is hot. Getting them to say yes to 20 is the goal. Asking for an hour at the first meeting raises the barrier.
  • After you ask — go completely silent. This is the hardest discipline in cold calling. New reps fill the silence because it feels like something's wrong. Nothing is wrong. They're thinking. Let them think. The first person who speaks after the ask is in the weaker position.
  • Practice silence with a partner. Have them ask you for something and practice sitting in silence for 5 full seconds. Record and time it. 5 seconds in a real call feels like 30 seconds. You have to train for this specifically.
  • What you're asking for is a conversation — not a sale. Frame it that way. "I'm not asking you to buy anything — I'm asking for 20 minutes to see if there's a conversation worth having."
Step 5 — Handle the brush-off: the first "no" is almost never real
Acknowledge, redirect, ask once more — then respect
"Not interested" response
"I completely understand — most people I speak with say the same thing before we've had a chance to connect. All I'm asking for is 20 minutes. If it's not a fit, I'll tell you myself. Does [specific day] work?"
"Send me some info" response
"Happy to. So I send you exactly the right thing — what's most relevant for you right now: [option A] or [option B]? And let me grab a time to walk through it with you — does [day] or [other day] work?"
"I'm too busy" response
"I respect your time — that's exactly why I keep this to 20 minutes. Is [specific day] better or [other specific day]?"
"We already have a vendor" response
"Great — most of the companies I work with had someone too. They brought me in because [specific gap or reason]. Worth 20 minutes to see if there's something we could add?"
The discipline of step 5
  • Never argue with the brush-off. Acknowledge it first, every single time. "I understand" or "I hear you" before anything else. Acknowledgment is not agreement — it's emotional credibility.
  • Never say "but" after acknowledging. "I understand, but..." negates everything that came before it. Use "and" or pause before your redirect. "I completely understand. Most people tell me the same thing. All I'm asking for is 20 minutes."
  • You get exactly two asks per call. Not three. Two. If they decline twice, thank them professionally and end the call. Never beg. Never argue. Never guilt-trip. Dignity in every call — even the ones that say no.
  • The "send me info" trap: Never just send info and wait. That's 95% dead. Always convert it to an appointment: "I'll send that right after our call — let me also grab a time to walk through it with you. Does [day] work?"
  • Drill your 8 most common brush-offs with a partner until your response comes within 2 seconds, sounds natural, and ends with an ask. Hesitation in step 5 is fatal. Fluency converts.

Voice & tonality — the hidden close

In a cold call, you have no face, no body language, no visual presence. You have one asset: your voice. How you use it determines whether prospects stay on the line, like you, trust you, and agree to meet. This is not optional polish — it is the core skill of telephone selling.

"On the phone, your voice IS your handshake, your suit, your office, and your smile. Master it and you master the call."

— Jeb Blount
The 5 dimensions of voice you must master
Record yourself on every session until all 5 are natural
Pace
  • Speak 20% slower than you think you need to. Nervous reps rush. Slow pace signals confidence.
  • Slow down on key words: your company name, their company name, your ask. These words must land clearly.
  • Match their pace after rapport is established — pacing too slow when they speak fast creates friction. Mirror gradually.
Volume & energy
  • Project slightly more volume than a normal conversation. You want to sound awake, alert, and worth listening to.
  • Stand up to increase natural volume and energy. Sitting slumped reduces both measurably.
  • Drop volume slightly when you're about to say something important — it creates emphasis through contrast.
Inflection
  • Flat delivery kills calls. Vary your pitch — go up on interesting points, come down on confident statements.
  • Avoid upward inflection on statements. "I work with tech companies?" sounds uncertain. "I work with tech companies." — period. End your sentences going down when you're stating something confident.
  • Use a slight upward inflection on questions to signal genuine curiosity, not interrogation.
Strategic silence
  • Use silence after your ask. 3–5 seconds minimum. Train yourself to sit in it without filling it.
  • Use silence after they say something interesting: "Hmm..." [2 second pause] signals you're thinking, not scripted.
  • Silence after an objection signals confidence, not confusion. It buys you thinking time and shows you're not rattled.
Warmth — the most underestimated dimension
  • Smile before you dial. Your larynx literally changes shape when you smile — your voice sounds warmer, more trustworthy, more approachable. Record yourself both ways and compare. The difference is dramatic.
  • Genuine curiosity warms your voice. Before you dial, think: "I'm genuinely curious about this person's business." Not scripted interest — real interest. It changes how you sound.
  • Say the prospect's first name once near the start and once near the close. People respond to their name. More than once feels manipulative. Twice feels personal.

The voice training protocol — how to build these skills fast
Daily practice · 30 days to measurable improvement
The daily recording practice
  • Record every calling session. Most diallers allow this. If not, use a second device. Listen to 3–5 calls after each session. This is uncomfortable. Do it anyway — it's the fastest feedback loop that exists.
  • What to listen for: filler words, pace (too fast?), inflection on statements (going up when it should go down?), silence after the ask (did you hold it?), warmth (did you sound like someone you'd want to talk to?).
  • Daily warm-up: Before your first block, record yourself reading your script standing up, smiling, at the pace you want on real calls. Play it back. Adjust. Dial.
  • Partner drill (weekly): One person plays prospect, one plays rep. Throw random objections. Rep must hold frame, acknowledge, redirect, ask again — in 30 seconds. Time it. 15 minutes per week of this is worth 50 hours of cold calling experience.
  • Listen to elite reps: Sales Gravy YouTube channel. Listen to Jeb's own cold calls. Study the pacing, the silences, the warmth. Then replicate it deliberately, not by accident.

Voicemail & email — multiplying your touch points

Most reps treat voicemail as a graveyard. Elite reps treat it as a weapon. Combined with a strategic email sequence, voicemail doubles your callback rate and warms every subsequent call. Here's how to make every single message count.

The power voicemail formula — under 30 seconds always
Every second over 30 reduces callback rate
Voicemail template A — Pain-based
"Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Company]. I'm calling because we've been working with [relevant type of company] to [specific outcome], and I thought it might be worth a conversation. I'll try you again [specific day], but if you'd prefer to reach me first, I'm at [number] — [repeat number slowly]. Again, [Your Name], [Company]. Talk soon."
Voicemail template B — Curiosity-based
"Hi [Name], [Your Name] with [Company]. I have some information I think would be genuinely relevant to what you're working on at [their company]. I'll call back [specific day] — or if you'd prefer to talk sooner, I'm at [number] — [number slowly]. Looking forward to it."
Voicemail template C — Referral or trigger
"Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] from [Company]. [Mutual contact] suggested I reach out — [one sentence why]. I'll try you again [specific day], but you can also reach me at [number] — [repeat slowly]. Thanks, [Your Name]."
The 7 rules of an elite voicemail
  • Under 30 seconds, always. Time every voicemail. If you go over, they will delete it before you finish. Practice until you land under 28 seconds every time.
  • State your number twice. Once at a normal speaking pace, once slowly. Most people don't replay voicemails — they write the number down while it's playing. If they miss it, you've lost the callback opportunity.
  • Sound confident and busy. You're a professional with valuable information, not someone hoping for a callback. Your tone should say "I have something worth your time" not "please call me back."
  • Always tell them when you'll call back: "I'll try you again Thursday." This primes them for your next call (Thursday's call is now warmer), and signals persistence without desperation.
  • Never ask them to "give you a call when you get a chance." This creates zero urgency. Give them a specific reason that matters to them, and then tell them you'll follow up.
  • Vary your voicemail message across touchpoints. If they hear the same message 3 times, they'll assume it's automated and stop listening. Each voicemail should have a different angle or reason for calling.
  • Leave voicemails at the end of your calling block. Don't use Golden Hours for voicemail-only rounds. Make live call attempts first, then leave voicemails for no-answers at the end.

  • 5 sentences maximum. Long emails don't get read by busy executives. Emails that look easy to reply to, do. Keep it short enough to read in one breath.
  • Subject line specificity is everything. "Quick question re: your Q3 hiring plans" beats "Partnership opportunity." Generic subjects get deleted. Specific, relevant subjects get opened.
  • The email's only job is to get a reply or warm up your next call. It is not a pitch. Do not try to sell in an email. Sell the conversation, not the product.
  • Always reference your last call attempt: "I called you Monday — you may have seen my number." This converts a cold email into a follow-up, which is dramatically more effective.
  • Follow up on every email with a call reference on your next dial: "I sent you an email Wednesday about [subject] — did it reach you?" This creates a warm bridge into the call.
  • Email is multi-channel ammunition — not a replacement for calls. If you're emailing instead of calling, you're avoiding. Email supports your calls; calls don't support your emails.

Objection handling — the 5-step turnaround

You cannot argue a prospect out of an objection. The harder you push, the deeper they dig in. The only way through is to acknowledge, get curious, and gently redirect. Objections are not walls — they're doors. Here is exactly how to open them.

"The number one reason salespeople fail to convert objections is that they respond before they understand. Listen first. Understand second. Respond third."

— Jeb Blount
1
Pause & breathe
2 full seconds. Don't react. Let your brain catch up to your mouth.
2
Acknowledge
"I completely understand." / "That makes a lot of sense." Not "but" — use "and" or pause.
3
Clarify
"Help me understand — when you say [X], what specifically concerns you?" Find the real objection.
4
Respond
Address the REAL objection — not the surface one. One strong point, not five weak ones.
5
Ask again
"Given that, does it make sense to spend 20 minutes together?" Max two asks total. Then respect it.
"Not interested."
✓ What to say
"I completely understand — most people I talk to feel the same way before we've had a chance to connect. All I'm asking for is 20 minutes. If it's not a fit, I'll tell you myself. Does [specific day] work?"
Why this works: You normalise their resistance ("most people"), reduce the ask ("20 minutes"), remove the risk ("I'll tell you"), and go straight to the calendar. You never argued the "not interested" — you bypassed it.
"Send me some information."
✓ What to say
"Happy to. So I send you exactly the right thing — what's most relevant to you right now: [option A] or [option B]? And let me grab a time to walk through it with you — does [day] or [other day] work?"
Why this works: You agree (removes friction), ask a qualifying question (shows you're not generic), and immediately convert the info send into an appointment. Never send info and wait. That's 95% dead. Always pair the send with a calendar ask.
"I'm too busy right now."
✓ What to say
"I completely respect that — that's exactly why I keep this to 20 minutes. Is [specific day] better or [other specific day]? I'll make sure we're out in 20."
Why this works: You validate their time constraint, then use it as your proof point. "20 minutes" is the answer to "too busy." You're not ignoring the objection — you're solving it.
"We already have a vendor / we're happy with who we use."
✓ What to say
"That's great to hear — most of the companies I work with had someone they were happy with too. They brought me in because [specific gap your solution fills]. Worth 20 minutes to see if there's something we could add to what you already have?"
Why this works: You validate their loyalty ("great to hear"), normalise the situation ("most companies"), identify a specific gap, and frame it as additive — not a rip-and-replace, which is threatening.
"We don't have budget for this."
✓ What to say
"I hear you — budget is always a real consideration. Help me understand: is budget a constraint right now, or is it more that you haven't seen enough to justify exploring it? I'm not asking you to spend anything — just 20 minutes to see if this is even relevant."
Why this works: Budget objections early in a cold call are almost always not about budget — they're about trust and relevance. This response clarifies whether it's a real constraint or a brush-off, and refocuses on zero-risk exploration.
"Call me back in 6 months / not right now."
✓ What to say
"Absolutely. Can I ask — is there something specific happening around that time that makes it a better moment? [Listen.] Got it. I'll put [specific date] in my calendar and reach out then. Can I send you something brief in the meantime to keep you posted?"
Why this works: You get intel on their timeline, you book a firm callback date in their awareness, and you stay in the picture with a low-threat touchpoint. Never just say "sure, talk then" — that call in 6 months will be cold again.
"I'm not the decision-maker."
✓ What to say
"Thanks for telling me — who would be the right person to speak with about [specific topic]? And would you be comfortable making a brief introduction, or should I reach out to them directly?"
Why this works: You don't argue or try to convince them to become the champion. You get the name, and you give them a graceful way to help you — an introduction. Many will do it. And now you have a warm referral instead of a cold call.
"I've never heard of your company."
✓ What to say
"Completely understandable — we're not everywhere yet. We work specifically with [your niche], and we've done [specific result] for companies like [reference]. That's actually why I'm calling — 20 minutes might change that. Does [day] or [day] work?"
Why this works: You acknowledge the gap in awareness (don't be defensive), pivot to specificity and a credible result, and reframe the unfamiliarity as an opportunity rather than a liability.

Gatekeeper mastery — getting to the decision-maker

The gatekeeper is not your enemy. They are a professional doing their job. The reps who fight gatekeepers lose. The reps who respect, engage, and earn their way through — win. Here is the complete playbook.

The mindset shift — gatekeepers are allies, not obstacles
Change this first or no tactic will work
How to think about and treat gatekeepers
  • Never be rude, impatient, or dismissive. Gatekeepers talk to their executives constantly. A rude rep is reported. A respectful, professional rep is sometimes put through or given direct information.
  • Learn their name. Call back and use it: "Hi Sarah, it's [Your Name] again — I spoke with you last week." Name recognition creates a relationship that benefits you over time.
  • Be straightforward about who you are. Trying to sneak through, being deceptive about your purpose, or pretending to have a relationship you don't have — these all backfire and burn the account.
  • Treat them as the important professional they are: "I know you're protecting [executive's] time — I appreciate that. I'll be brief about why I'm calling."
Gatekeeper scripts — word for word
Six scenarios with exact language
When they ask "What's this regarding?"
"I'm calling to speak with [Name] about [specific, business-relevant topic]. Is he/she available?"

← Be specific. Vague answers increase friction. Don't say "it's personal" — that's deceptive and they know it.
When they ask "Are you selling something?"
"I work with [type of company] on [specific area]. I have some information I believe would be relevant to [executive's name]. Is he/she available for a brief call?"

← Don't deny what you do. Reframe it around value and relevance, not selling.
When they say "He's not available, can I take a message?"
"Yes, please tell him/her [Your Name] from [Company] called about [specific topic]. I'll also try again [specific day]. What's the best time to reach him/her directly?"

← Always ask for the best time. You'll often get it. This turns your next call into a warm, expected contact.
When they say "He doesn't take cold calls"
"I completely understand — I'll keep it very brief. This is specifically about [very specific relevant topic]. Could you let him/her know I called? I'll try again [day]."

← Don't argue. Accept graciously, leave a specific message, commit to calling back. Persistence over time is your weapon.
Advanced gatekeeper strategies
  • Call before 8am or after 5:30pm. Executives often answer their own phones outside of business hours. Gatekeepers are not there. This is the most effective tactic for hard-to-reach executives.
  • Get the direct dial: "Is there a direct number I can reach [Name] on in the future?" You'll get it more often than you'd expect, especially after you've established rapport with the gatekeeper.
  • Call from a different number: Some gatekeepers screen your number. Using a mobile or different office line resets the screen. Not deception — just practical.
  • LinkedIn bypass: Many executives who are unreachable by phone respond to a well-crafted LinkedIn message. Use this as an additional channel, not a replacement for calling.
  • Build a relationship over time: If you call a gatekeeper repeatedly across weeks, they start to recognise you. Consistent, professional persistence sometimes leads them to advocate for you: "I've been speaking with [rep] — he seems legitimate, would you like me to put him through next time?"

Sales EQ — emotional intelligence as competitive edge

Technical skill gets you in the door. Emotional intelligence closes it. The reps who consistently outperform their peers aren't always the best at product knowledge or even technique — they're the ones who make prospects feel understood, respected, and valued. That's a trainable skill.

🔍
Self-awareness
Know your emotional triggers. What makes you rush? What makes you over-explain? Fear of silence? Annoyance when interrupted? These patterns cost you calls and you don't even know it.
🧘
Self-management
When a prospect is rude, your instinct is to match their energy or get defensive. Do the opposite: get calmer, warmer, and slower. Non-complementary behaviour disarms aggression.
👁️
Social awareness
On every call, read the signals: pace changes, tone drops, sighs, short answers. When you hear hesitation, stop pitching and ask what's on their mind. The real conversation starts there.
🤝
Relationship management
Every call ends with a defined next step. Every interaction leaves them feeling heard. Remember personal details. Reference them next time. People buy from people who make them feel known.
Likeability — your most underestimated asset on a cold call
The first 30 seconds decide 80% of the outcome
Specific actions that make you likeable instantly
  • Smile before you dial. The difference in your voice is measurable. Record yourself both ways and listen to the contrast. Use the smiling version every single time.
  • Use their name strategically: once near the start ("Hi [Name]!") and once near the close ("Given what you've told me, [Name]..."). More than twice sounds manipulative. Twice feels personal and warm.
  • Be genuinely curious about them. Ask one question about their world before you pitch anything: "What's your biggest challenge with [relevant area] right now?" Then actually listen to the full answer without interrupting or steering.
  • Never talk over them. The moment you interrupt, you break trust. If they start talking, stop. Even if they're saying something you desperately want to respond to. Let them finish completely.
  • Match their vocabulary. If they use technical jargon, match it. If they speak simply, simplify. Mismatched vocabulary creates unconscious distance. Matched vocabulary creates unconscious connection.
  • Validate their experience: "That makes a lot of sense given what you're dealing with." Not flattery — genuine validation of their reality. People relax when they feel understood.
Handling rejection without letting it compound
The mental discipline of high-volume cold calling
The reset protocol — after rejection streaks
  • Rejection is neutral data. They rejected the timing, the interruption, the moment — not you. You are not your call. Repeat this until it's automatic.
  • After a string of rejections, physically reset: Stand up, shake out your hands and arms, take 3 deep breaths. This is neurological pattern-interruption. It works. It is not optional.
  • Never carry the energy of one call into the next. When you hang up on a rejection, that call is over. The next call has zero knowledge of what just happened. Dial fresh every time.
  • Track your "record rejection streak." If your worst day had 12 consecutive rejections before a connect, knowing that makes the 8th rejection feel manageable instead of catastrophic. Context reduces suffering.
  • The antidote to a bad block is more dials, not fewer. When a block is going badly, the instinct is to stop. Do the opposite. Stay in the block. Your statistics will normalise over volume. Five bad calls followed by two great ones still make you money.

Defining your Ideal Customer Profile (ICP)
  • Define before you list-build. Industry? Company size (employees or revenue)? Geography? Title of decision-maker? Typical pain points? Every prospect on your list must match this profile — no exceptions.
  • Without a defined ICP, you're broadcasting noise. With one, every dial hits someone who has the problem you solve. The difference in conversion rates is dramatic.

Purging and maintaining pipeline hygiene
  • Remove stale prospects after 8 unanswered attempts across all channels over 3 weeks. Do not hold them in your pipeline — they inflate your numbers and distort your conversion ratios.

The exact review process
  • Pull your weekly numbers: total dials, total connects, total conversations, appointments set, appointments kept, proposals sent.
